# Draft queue and recovery test matrix

This matrix closes the practice gaps called out in [the readiness report](DRAFT_READINESS.md), [the launch evidence](PRACTICE_LAUNCH_2026-10-10.md), and [the rehearsal report](PRACTICE_REHEARSAL_2026-10-10.md). The coordinator and browser adapter are tested offline; the rows marked **Browser** require the root operator to use the visible ESPN room. The room, session, timer, and exact visible IDs must be recorded with every browser result.

## Safety boundary

The local coordinator's **Pause** stops new FBL browser mutations. It cancels an undispatched local action and blocks `prepare`, `claim`, `final_guard`, and direct submission. It cannot retract a queue entry that was already submitted to ESPN. ESPN may consume an existing native queue entry when the clock expires while the local coordinator is paused. That outcome is a native ESPN side effect, not a local click after Pause.

The allowed paused-queue tests are therefore:

1. For a local cancel test, clear ESPN's queue first, pause locally, and prove that no new FBL action is dispatched and no queue entry appears.
2. For native timeout consumption, deliberately queue known players through the visible ESPN controls, then leave the local coordinator paused and let ESPN's clock consume the queue. Record the resulting history and roster as evidence that native queue consumption is independent of local Pause.
3. After any native queue consumption, stop the test, publish the resulting frame, reconcile the room, and explicitly resume only after Autopick, history, roster, turn, and clock checks pass. Do not claim that local Pause prevented the queued pick.

Do not use a live-room player or an uncontrolled opponent turn for a queue experiment. Use a fresh practice room, stable `button[data-player-id]` identities, and a disposable test pick. Queue setup and removal are visible ESPN mutations owned by the root operator; this audit does not operate ESPN.

## Observed browser pilot

In the October 11 practice pilot, Shai was queued before Jokic with Autopick explicitly off and a 15-second native timer. On timeout ESPN selected Shai, and the history and roster agreed. ESPN then enabled Autopick; after the operator paused the native room and turned Autopick off, Jokic was later gone from the queue because an opponent had selected him. This is evidence for first-entry timeout consumption and for reading back the fate of later queue entries; it does not show that local coordinator Pause can stop ESPN's native clock or queue behavior.

The same practice room then supplied recovery evidence. The coordinator started at pick 18; status polling let the observation age past ten seconds, and the coordinator entered `stale_pause`. Publishing a fresh frame at pick 28 left it paused, as required. The root operator enabled manual override, staged Siakam, and confirmed that `prepare` was rejected with `automation is not running`. The exact staged player was then selected through manual browser control; history and roster both showed pick 28, and `confirm-manual-pick` marked the staged action confirmed. ESPN was paused at pick 29, the browser was reloaded, and room 195 retained history through pick 28, two roster players, and Autopick off while the coordinator remained paused. After an explicit native Resume and coordinator Resume, automated Bam at pick 29 completed prepare-to-click in 1,166 ms and click-to-confirmation in 2,197 ms, with the action confirmed. This passes the stale recovery, deliberate manual takeover, reload/reconnect persistence, explicit dual resume, and post-recovery direct-pick gates for that practice room.

## Operator evidence record

For each browser row, record:

`test_id`, UTC start/end, practice room league ID, coordinator session ID, observed revision before setup and after recovery, timer setting and visible seconds, queued player IDs in intended and observed order, queue empty-state text, Autopick checked state, local state/reason, action IDs/statuses, history pick number/team/player ID, roster player ID, and the final disposition (`pass`, `fail`, or `unverified`).

For Q2 explicitly record that local Pause remained active while ESPN consumed the pre-existing queue entry. For Q4 explicitly distinguish a local action cancellation from a queue item removal; they are separate systems and must not be reported as one behavior.
