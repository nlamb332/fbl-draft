## Findings

- The minutes fit is materially better in-sample (R^2 and bias) and cuts MIN bias from about +2.0 to +0.3 overall, but it overpredicts the top tier and centers; naive prior-season minutes remain the best minutes projection when one exists, so the imputation matters only when the forecast has no MIN.
- Calibrated PF/36 does **not** beat the legacy rates out-of-sample: foul rates fell in the test seasons and per-36 rates are lower for high-minute players, so pooled rates overpredict stars (tier T1). Treat the PF result as inconclusive, which is why the flag stays off and `pf_scale` bands should be kept.
- The TD scale (>1) shows the independent-tail model understates triple-doubles, as its docstring predicted; out-of-sample, the scaled TD rate cuts bias (T1 +0.40 vs -0.73 per 100 games) but slightly raises MAE, so it is not decisive either.

## Caveats

- Prior-season actuals are the only public stand-in for projections; real preseason projections would carry extra error, so these are a lower bound on projection error.
- Basketball-Reference positions are not ESPN eligibility. Eligibility classes C/G and C/F/G have no analogue and keep their legacy PF rates.
- Rookies, injured players and sub-20-game seasons are excluded, which flatters the minutes model.
- The checked-in synthetic-data promotion is not re-run here; re-validate before enabling the flag.
